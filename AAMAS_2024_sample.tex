\author{Arnaud Gardille}

\begin{abstract}
In off-policy multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), each agent perceives
the other learners as part of its environment; as their policies drift, the
perceived dynamics become non-stationary and previously collected transitions
turn misleading. The usual remedy---shrinking the replay buffer---discards the
sample efficiency that motivates off-policy learning. We recast the
\emph{staleness} of a stored transition as its \emph{conformity} to the other
agents' \emph{current} policies and study it with conformal prediction: a
distribution-free non-conformity score, calibrated on fresh transitions into
$p$-values, measuring how surprising a transition is under the present (or, when
policies are unavailable, under a model of the currently perceived dynamics).
Three findings follow. \emph{Theory}: the value off-policy MARL targets is a
perceived best-response value, and reweighting replay towards
currently-compatible transitions computes the best response of a
\emph{support-starved} perceived MDP---so naively \emph{filtering} for
compatibility can move the fixed point away from the optimum by starving
exploration, which we confirm empirically. \emph{Certified replay}: where the
importance ratio is available, $\rho$-weighted conformal and time-decay
calibration \emph{correct} the replay distribution with finite-sample guarantees
instead of filtering it. \emph{Detection} (our most robust contribution): a
conformal test martingale flags when the perceived environment has drifted, with
an \emph{anytime} false-alarm guarantee via Ville's inequality, and drives a
two-channel controller whose frozen alarm preserves the guarantee while an
adaptive channel reads the drift rate; on standard cooperative benchmarks it
detects co-learner drift faster than a TD-error heuristic at matched false-alarm,
and the same martingale \emph{certifies} the replay correction. We study the
approach on controlled environments built to isolate perceived non-stationarity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many domains---robotics, logistics, healthcare, finance, and strategy
games---could benefit from agents that learn to act together. Yet, in spite of
impressive results on benchmarks such as the StarCraft Multi-Agent
Challenge~\cite{samvelyan2019starcraft}, multi-agent reinforcement learning
(MARL) remains hard to deploy because of slow and unstable
convergence~\cite{matignon2012independent}.

A large part of this difficulty has a single, well-identified cause. Single-agent
reinforcement learning assumes a \emph{stationary} environment. In MARL, from the
point of view of one agent, the other agents are part of the environment; as they
update their policies, the dynamics that the agent perceives \emph{change over
time}. This \emph{perceived non-stationarity}~\cite{matignon2012independent} is
particularly damaging for \emph{off-policy}, value-based methods, which owe their
sample efficiency to a \emph{replay buffer}: they repeatedly learn from
transitions collected long ago, under \emph{outdated} policies of the other
agents. A common workaround is to keep the buffer small, so that stale
transitions are quickly forgotten---but this throws away exactly the sample
efficiency and the offline-learning capability that motivate off-policy learning.

The key observation of this paper is that \emph{old does not imply useless}. A
stored transition is harmful only insofar as the joint action taken by the other
agents in it is \emph{implausible under their current policies}. If their stored
behaviour is still compatible with what they would do now, the transition is as
informative as a fresh one. The problem of reusing the past therefore becomes one
of \emph{measuring each transition's conformity to the present}---and, as we will
show, using that signal to \emph{detect} when the buffer has drifted and to
\emph{correct} the replay distribution, rather than merely to \emph{filter} it,
which can backfire by starving exploration.

Prior work makes this concrete through an importance-sampling ratio between the
current and past probabilities of the other agents'
actions~\cite{foerster2017stabilising}. We revisit that idea, fix its
mathematical formulation, and show that the ratio is often either
\emph{unavailable} (partial observability, continuous actions, parametric
exploration, black-box teammates) or \emph{numerically unstable} (its variance
explodes). We then replace the explicit ratio by a \emph{calibrated,
distribution-free measure of conformity} drawn from conformal
prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Theory.} We formalise the value off-policy MARL implicitly targets
  as a \emph{perceived best-response value} under a quasi-stationary approximation
  (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}, Appendix~\ref{app:br}), and prove a
  \emph{support-starvation} result: reweighting replay towards currently-compatible
  transitions computes the best response of a support-\emph{starved} perceived MDP,
  matching the true best response only for exact-ratio soft weights with full
  support---so naive compatibility \emph{filtering} can move the fixed point away
  from the optimum (Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation}).
  \item \textbf{Certified replay.} We give a corrected account of importance-ratio
  reweighting (change-of-measure and its support condition, the heavy-tailed
  instability of direct loss correction, and the self-normalised nature of ratio
  prioritisation), and turn it into \emph{calibrated} corrections with
  finite-sample validity---$\rho$-weighted conformal $p$-values and time-decay
  calibration---that \emph{correct} the replay distribution rather than merely
  filter it (Sections~\ref{sec:formulation}, \ref{sec:certified}).
  \item \textbf{Detection (headline).} We introduce a conformal \emph{test
  martingale} that detects drift of the perceived environment with an \emph{anytime}
  false-alarm guarantee via Ville's inequality, and a two-channel controller whose
  frozen channel preserves that guarantee while an adaptive channel reads the drift
  rate (Section~\ref{sec:detection}). The same martingale \emph{certifies} the
  replay correction---its growth rate bounds the selector's coverage gap
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:certify}).
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} On a diagnostic bandit and standard cooperative
  JaxMARL benchmarks, compatibility \emph{selection} starves exploration (confirming
  Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation}), whereas \emph{detection} flags co-learner drift
  faster than a TD-error heuristic at matched false-alarm
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Notation}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Reinforcement learning}
A Markov Decision Process (MDP) is a tuple $(S,A,P,R,\gamma)$ with state set $S$,
action set $A$, transition kernel $P(\cdot\mid s,a)\in\Delta(S)$, reward
distribution $R(\cdot\mid s,a)$, and discount $\gamma\in[0,1)$. A policy
$\pi:S\to\Delta(A)$ induces a return $\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t R_{t}$, and the
action-value function $Q_\pi(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_\pi[\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tR_t\mid
S_0=s,A_0=a]$. The optimal value $Q^*=\max_\pi Q_\pi$ is the unique fixed point
of the optimal Bellman operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bellman}
(\Bop^* Q)(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)}\Big[R(s,a,s')+\gamma
\max_{a'}Q(s',a')\Big],
\end{equation}
which is a $\gamma$-contraction for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$
(Appendix~\ref{app:contraction}). Note that the right-hand side uses $Q$, not
$Q^*$: $\Bop^*$ maps an arbitrary $Q$ to a new function, and iterating it
converges to $Q^*$ by Banach's theorem.

\paragraph{Deep $Q$-learning.}
DQN~\cite{mnih2013playing} parameterises $Q_\phi$ by a neural network, stores
transitions in a replay buffer $\Dcal$, and minimises, over uniformly sampled
batches,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dqn}
\mathcal{L}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{(s,a,r,s')\sim U(\Dcal)}\Big[\big(Q_\phi(s,a)-y\big)^2\Big],
\quad y=r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\bar\phi}(s',a'),
\end{equation}
with $\bar\phi$ a periodically updated target network. A stochastic gradient step
on a batch of size $B$ reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sgd}
\phi\;\leftarrow\;\phi-\alpha\,\nabla_\phi\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^{B}\big(y_i-Q_\phi(s_i,a_i)\big)^2 .
\end{equation}
Exploration typically uses an $\epsilon$-greedy policy: act greedily w.p.\
$1-\epsilon$ and uniformly at random w.p.\ $\epsilon$.

\subsection{Multi-agent reinforcement learning}
A cooperative \emph{stochastic game} is a tuple
$(N,S,(\mathbb{U}_a)_{a=1}^N,\mathcal{T},(r_a)_{a=1}^N,\gamma)$: $N$ agents share
the state $s\in S$, agent $a$ picks $u_a\in\mathbb{U}_a$, the joint action
$\mathbf{u}=(u_a,\uma)$ drives the transition
$\mathcal{T}(\cdot\mid s,\mathbf{u})$ and the reward $r_a(s,\mathbf{u})$. We
write $a$ for the agent under consideration and $-a$ for the others, so that
$u_a\sim\pi_a(\cdot\mid s)$ and $\uma\sim\pi_{-a}(\cdot\mid s)$.

\paragraph{Independent $Q$-learning (IQL).}
In IQL~\cite{tampuu2017multiagent} each agent runs its own DQN, treating the
others as part of the environment. Its loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iql}
\mathcal{L}_a(\phi_a)=\mathbb{E}_{(s,\mathbf{u},r,s')\sim U(\Dcal)}
\Big[\big(Q_{\phi_a}(s,u_a)-y_a\big)^2\Big],
\end{equation}
\[
y_a=r_a+\gamma\max_{u_a'}Q_{\bar\phi_a}(s',u_a').
\]
IQL is simple and, despite its non-stationarity, underlies competitive
value-decomposition methods such as VDN~\cite{sunehag2018value} and
QMIX~\cite{rashid2018qmix}; the mechanisms we develop apply to those methods
unchanged, since each agent still maintains an individual $Q$-function. We adopt
the fully observable, simultaneous-action setting for clarity; the partially
observable case is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\section{The Replay Mismatch in MARL}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{The perceived MDP and the best-response value}
Fix agent $a$ and freeze the others at a joint policy $\pi_{-a}$. From $a$'s
viewpoint the game collapses to an MDP $\mathcal{M}_a(\pi_{-a})$ with
\emph{perceived} transition and reward kernels
\begin{align}
P^{\pi_{-a}}(s'\mid s,u_a)&=\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\pi_{-a}(\cdot\mid s)}\big[\mathcal{T}(s'\mid s,u_a,\uma)\big],\\
\bar r^{\pi_{-a}}(s,u_a)&=\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\pi_{-a}(\cdot\mid s)}\big[r_a(s,u_a,\uma)\big].
\end{align}
The value that IQL implicitly chases is the optimal value of this perceived MDP,
i.e.\ the \emph{best response} of agent $a$ to $\pi_{-a}$, defined as the fixed
point of the best-response Bellman operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br}
(\Bop^{\mathrm{BR}}_{\pi_{-a}}Q)(s,u_a)=
\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\pi_{-a}(\cdot\mid s),\,s'\sim\mathcal{T}}
\Big[r_a(s,\mathbf{u})+\gamma\max_{u_a'}Q(s',u_a')\Big].
\end{equation}
We denote its fixed point $\Qbr(s,u_a;\pi_{-a})$. For \emph{fixed} $\pi_{-a}$,
$\Bop^{\mathrm{BR}}_{\pi_{-a}}$ is a $\gamma$-contraction
(Appendix~\ref{app:br}), so $\Qbr(\cdot;\pi_{-a})$ is well defined.

This is a \emph{best-response} value, \emph{not} a joint-optimal or equilibrium
value: it is optimal for $a$ \emph{only against the fixed} $\pi_{-a}$. During
training the others keep learning, so the relevant target is
$\Qbr(\cdot;\picur)$ with $\picur$ \emph{moving}. Off-policy MARL thus rests on a
\emph{quasi-stationary} approximation: at each update we treat the current others'
policies as fixed. Perceived non-stationarity is precisely the drift of this
target, and it is what makes a fixed buffer inconsistent.

\subsection{What exactly is mismatched}
The buffer stores transitions $\tau_i=(s_i,u_{i,a},\umai,r_i,s_i')$ in which the
others' actions were drawn from their \emph{past} policy,
$\umai\sim\pipasti(\cdot\mid s_i)$. The loss we would \emph{like} to minimise
draws them from the \emph{current} policy:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:loss-ideal}
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{cur}}_a(\phi_a)&=
\mathbb{E}_{\,\uma\sim\picur(\cdot\mid s)}\Big[\big(Q_{\phi_a}(s,u_a)-y_a\big)^2\Big],\\
\label{eq:loss-real}
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{past}}_a(\phi_a)&=
\mathbb{E}_{\,\uma\sim\pipast(\cdot\mid s)}\Big[\big(Q_{\phi_a}(s,u_a)-y_a\big)^2\Big],
\end{align}
where $s$ and $u_a$ are drawn from the buffer in both lines, while the target
$y_a$, the reward $r$ and the next state $s'$ are \emph{downstream} of $\uma$
(since $r=r_a(s,u_a,\uma)$ and $s'\sim\mathcal{T}(\cdot\mid s,u_a,\uma)$).
Equations~\eqref{eq:loss-ideal} and \eqref{eq:loss-real} thus differ only through
the conditional law of $\uma$ given $s$; the environment's conditional law of
$(r,s')$ given $(s,u_a,\uma)$ is policy-independent, which is exactly why a single
change of measure on $\uma$ (below) suffices. In particular, the \emph{state}
distribution is, in both cases, that of the
buffer: reweighting the others' actions does \emph{not} turn the past
state-visitation $d^{\mathrm{past}}(s)$ into the current one
$d^{\mathrm{cur}}(s)$. Any action-level correction therefore fixes
\emph{which joint actions} are credited, not \emph{which states} are visited---a
distinction that matters under function approximation and that we make explicit
rather than hide.

\subsection{Importance reweighting, done correctly}
\label{sec:ratio}
Going from \eqref{eq:loss-real} to \eqref{eq:loss-ideal} is a change of measure.
For any integrable $f$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:is}
\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\picur(\cdot\mid s)}[f]=
\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\pipast(\cdot\mid s)}\!\left[\rho\,f\right],
\qquad
\rho=\frac{\picur(\uma\mid s)}{\pipast(\uma\mid s)},
\end{equation}
\emph{provided the support condition}
$\supp\picur(\cdot\mid s)\subseteq\supp\pipast(\cdot\mid s)$ holds. With
unmasked $\epsilon$-greedy exploration every action keeps positive probability,
so $\rho$ is finite; but when actions are \emph{masked} (as in our Water-Bomber
environment) the past support can miss actions that are likely now, and $\rho$
diverges. This is not a numerical detail---it is where the method breaks.

\paragraph{Direct loss correction is unbiased but unstable.}
Multiplying each squared TD error by $\rho_i$ yields an unbiased estimate of
\eqref{eq:loss-ideal}. Unfortunately $\rho$ is a ratio of products of small
probabilities over several agents; it is heavy-tailed, and a single large
$\rho_i$ produces a gradient spike. This is the empirically observed
instability~\cite{foerster2017stabilising} that we revisit in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Prioritisation is self-normalised, not unbiased.}
Instead of multiplying the loss, one can \emph{sample} transition $i$ with
probability $P(i)=\rho_i/\sum_k\rho_k$ and drop the explicit factor. The
resulting estimator of $\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\picur}[f]$ is the
\emph{self-normalised} importance estimator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:snis}
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{SNIS}}=\frac{\sum_i\rho_i f_i}{\sum_k\rho_k}
\;\neq\;\frac1N\sum_i\rho_i f_i=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{IS}} .
\end{equation}
The two coincide asymptotically when $\mathbb{E}[\rho]=1$, but the
self-normalised one is \emph{biased at finite batch size}. Crucially, the
recommendation \emph{not} to divide back by $P(i)$ (unlike prioritised experience
replay~\cite{schaul2016prioritized}) is \emph{not} a variance trick that
preserves the objective: it is a \emph{deliberate change of the optimised
objective} towards the current-policy expectation. Stating this honestly removes
the apparent contradiction between ``we correct the expectation'' and ``we change
the distribution without correction'': we do the latter, on purpose.

\paragraph{Why the explicit ratio is not enough.}
Computing $\rho_i$ requires (i)~storing or recomputing $\pipasti(\umai\mid s_i)$
and (ii)~evaluating $\picur(\umai\mid s_i)$. This is feasible only for
\emph{queryable, discrete} policies with shared support. It fails for partially
observable agents (whose policies condition on histories), continuous actions
(densities, not masses), parametric or noisy exploration, and black-box or
human teammates. These are exactly the realistic cases. We now replace the ratio
by a quantity that survives all of them.

\section{Conformal Replay}
\label{sec:method}

The common object of this section is a \emph{non-conformity score} that grades how
incompatible a stored transition is with the other agents' current behaviour, and
its calibration into distribution-free $p$-values. The score then drives both a
\emph{selection} rule (Section~\ref{sec:selection}) and a \emph{detection} rule
(Section~\ref{sec:detection}).

\subsection{A non-conformity score for stored transitions}
\label{sec:score}
We seek a score $A(\tau)$ that is \emph{large} when transition $\tau$ is stale,
i.e.\ when its other-agent behaviour is unlikely under the present. We give
three, in decreasing order of the access to the other agents they require.

\paragraph{(A) Policy-based scores.} When the policies are queryable and the
others explore with discrete, full-support actions, the \emph{exact} score is the
log importance ratio
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score-ratio}
A(\tau_i)=-\log\rho_i=-\log\picur(\umai\mid s_i)+\log\pipasti(\umai\mid s_i):
\end{equation}
resampling transitions with weight $\rho_i$ realises the self-normalised
estimator~\eqref{eq:snis}, i.e.\ makes the replayed $\uma$ follow $\picur$, and
recovers the importance-weighting of \citet{foerster2017stabilising}. It requires
the \emph{past} likelihoods $\pipasti(\umai\mid s_i)$ to be stored with each
transition.

When only the \emph{current} policies can be queried, a cheaper, ratio-free
surrogate is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score-policy}
A(\tau_i)=-\log\picur(\umai\mid s_i),
\end{equation}
which scores high any stored joint action the others would now rarely take, needs
no past likelihood, and has no support/blow-up problem. It is, however, a
\emph{coarser heuristic}: unlike $-\log\rho$ it does not target $\picur$ exactly,
and it over-penalises actions that are intrinsically rare yet still current
(where $\picur\approx\pipast$ are both small, so $\rho\approx1$). It is the
fallback---together with the model-based score below---for the regimes (partial
observability, continuous actions, black-box teammates) where $\rho$ is
unavailable.

\paragraph{(B) Model-based score.} When the policies cannot be queried, learn a
model $\hat h^{\mathrm{cur}}(s',r\mid s,u_a)$ of the \emph{currently perceived}
dynamics from a sliding window of fresh transitions, and score a stored
transition by its surprise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score-model}
A(\tau_i)=-\log \hat h^{\mathrm{cur}}(s_i',r_i\mid s_i,u_{i,a}),
\end{equation}
or, for a mean predictor $\hat m^{\mathrm{cur}}$ with residual covariance
$\hat\Sigma$, by the Mahalanobis residual
$A(\tau_i)=\lVert s_i'-\hat m^{\mathrm{cur}}(s_i,u_{i,a})\rVert^2_{\hat\Sigma^{-1}}$.
Because the others' policies enter the perceived kernel
$P^{\picur}$, a change in $\picur$ shows up as elevated prediction error: stale
transitions are exactly the out-of-distribution ones. We deliberately do
\emph{not} assume the error has zero mean---a learned model is imperfect---and
calibrate its nominal distribution instead, as follows.

\subsection{From scores to conformal $p$-values}
\label{sec:pvalues}
Maintain a calibration set $\Ccal=\{\tau^{\mathrm{cal}}_j\}_{j=1}^n$ of
\emph{fresh} transitions, generated under the current policies and held out from
fitting the score. Let $A_j^{\mathrm{cal}}=A(\tau_j^{\mathrm{cal}})$. For a
buffer transition $\tau_i$ with score $A_i=A(\tau_i)$, define the (inductive)
conformal $p$-value
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pvalue}
p_i=\frac{1+\big|\{\,j:\;A_j^{\mathrm{cal}}\ge A_i\,\}\big|}{n+1}\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
A small $p_i$ means $\tau_i$ is more non-conforming than almost all fresh
transitions. Under the null hypothesis that $\tau_i$ is exchangeable with the
calibration sample (its other-agent behaviour conforms to the present), $p_i$ is
(super-)uniform on $(0,1]$~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic}. The construction is
\emph{distribution-free}: it inherits no Gaussian or zero-mean assumption from
the score. When precision matters, a Mondrian / state-conditional variant
restricts the calibration set to transitions whose $(s,u_a)$ falls in the same
region as $\tau_i$, which guards against the model being merely less accurate in
some part of the space.

\subsection{Use 1: Conformal transition selection}
\label{sec:selection}
We turn the $p$-values into a replay distribution. Following the large-batch
recipe of LaBER~\cite{lahire2022large}, we draw a large batch, score it, and
\emph{subsample} a training mini-batch with a probability that \emph{increases
with conformity}, e.g.\ proportional to $p_i$, or a hard conformal filter
$\indic{p_i\ge\alpha}$ at level $\alpha$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:select}). Intuitively
we keep transitions that the present could plausibly have produced.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Conformal Likely-Transition Selection (per agent $a$)}
\label{alg:select}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require buffer $\Dcal$, fresh calibration set $\Ccal$, large-batch size $L$,
mini-batch size $B$, score $A(\cdot)$
\While{training}
  \State refresh $\Ccal$ with recent on-policy transitions; compute
  $\{A^{\mathrm{cal}}_j\}$
  \State sample a large batch $\{\tau_i\}_{i=1}^{L}$ from $\Dcal$ (uniformly or
  with PER)
  \State compute scores $A_i=A(\tau_i)$ and $p$-values $p_i$ via \eqref{eq:pvalue}
  \State set weights $w_i\propto p_i$ (or $w_i\propto\indic{p_i\ge\alpha}$);
  subsample $B$ indices $\sim w$
  \State compute up-to-date targets $y_i$ and take an SGD step \eqref{eq:sgd} on
  the mini-batch
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Relation to the importance ratio.} With the exact score
\eqref{eq:score-ratio}, conformal selection recovers a \emph{rank-stabilised}
form of the importance prioritisation of \citet{foerster2017stabilising}: a high
$\rho$ gives a low $A$, hence a high conformity $p$-value and a high selection
weight. The difference is that the conformal calibration uses only the
\emph{rank} of $A_i$ among fresh transitions, so the scale of $\rho$---and its
heavy right tail (Section~\ref{sec:ratio})---no longer destabilises training. The
ratio-free score~\eqref{eq:score-policy} and the model-based score extend the
same rule to the cases where $\rho$ cannot be computed.

\paragraph{What is and is not corrected.} As in \eqref{eq:snis}, this selection
\emph{deliberately} reshapes the training distribution towards the current
perceived MDP; it is a calibrated heuristic reweighting, not an unbiased
estimator, and---like every action-level scheme---it does not correct the
state-visitation $d^{\mathrm{past}}(s)$. We do not over-claim beyond this.

\subsection{Use 2: Detecting drift of the perceived environment}
\label{sec:detection}
Selection decides \emph{which} transitions to trust; we also want to know
\emph{when} the buffer as a whole has gone stale, so as to refresh the
calibration set, down-weight or partially flush the buffer, or widen exploration.
This is a sequential change-detection problem on the stream of incoming
$p$-values, for which conformal \emph{test martingales} are the natural
tool~\cite{vovk2003testing,volkhonskiy2017inductive,ramdas2023gametheoretic}.

For a fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $p$-values $p_1,p_2,\dots$, define the
power martingale, and its mixture,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:martingale}
M_t^{\varepsilon}=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\varepsilon\,p_j^{\varepsilon-1},
\qquad
M_t=\int_0^1\prod_{j=1}^{t}\varepsilon\,p_j^{\varepsilon-1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon .
\end{equation}
If, under the null $H_0$ (no drift), the $p_j$ are conditionally super-uniform
given the past, then $(M_t)_{t\ge0}$ is a non-negative martingale with
$M_0=1$ and $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[M_t]=1$ (Appendix~\ref{app:martingale}). Ville's
inequality then bounds the \emph{anytime} false-alarm probability,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ville}
\mathbb{P}_{H_0}\!\left(\sup_{t\ge1}M_t\ge \tfrac1\delta\right)\le\delta,
\end{equation}
so raising an alarm when $M_t\ge1/\delta$ controls the probability of \emph{ever}
falsely declaring drift by $\delta$, with no need to fix a horizon
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:detect}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Conformal Drift Detection (per agent $a$)}
\label{alg:detect}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require calibration set $\Ccal$, level $\delta$, mixing over $\varepsilon$
\State $M\gets1$
\For{each newly observed transition $\tau_t$}
  \State compute $A_t=A(\tau_t)$ and $p_t$ via \eqref{eq:pvalue} against $\Ccal$
  \State $M\gets M\cdot\int_0^1\varepsilon\,p_t^{\varepsilon-1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon$
  \If{$M\ge1/\delta$}
    \State \textbf{declare drift}: refresh $\Ccal$, down-weight/flush $\Dcal$,
    reset $M\gets1$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Honest statement of guarantees.} The bound \eqref{eq:ville} is
\emph{exact} only when the $p_t$ are conditionally super-uniform under $H_0$. In
RL this requires care: transitions are temporally dependent and the agent's own
policy shapes the state distribution. We distinguish three regimes. \emph{(i)}
With an exact perceived model, or with inductive conformal calibration that
preserves exchangeability~\cite{volkhonskiy2017inductive}, conditional validity
holds and \eqref{eq:ville} is rigorous. \emph{(ii)} With a held-out
in-distribution calibration set we obtain marginal validity, which degrades if
deployment visits regions under-represented in calibration. \emph{(iii)}
Otherwise the threshold is tuned on nominal trajectories for an acceptable
empirical false-alarm rate. We report which regime we are in rather than claiming
the strongest one by default.

\subsection{Use 3: Certifying a replayed batch under a known shift}
\label{sec:certified}
The $p$-values of \eqref{eq:pvalue} are valid only while the calibration set is
exchangeable with the scored transitions. When the others' policies have
\emph{measurably} shifted---after the detector of Section~\ref{sec:detection}
fires, or whenever the ratio $\rho$ is itself available---we can \emph{certify}
validity rather than assume it, with two classical corrections that transfer
directly to the replay stream. \emph{(i) Covariate-shift weighting.} If the shift
is captured by a likelihood ratio $w=\rho=\picur/\pipast$, the \emph{weighted}
conformal $p$-value~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted-p}
p_i=\sum_{j}\tilde w_j\,\indic{A^{\mathrm{cal}}_j\ge A_i}+\tilde w_{\mathrm{test}},
\qquad
\tilde w_j=\frac{w_j}{\textstyle\sum_k w_k+w_{\mathrm{test}}},
\end{equation}
restores super-uniformity with respect to the \emph{shifted} (current) law.
\emph{(ii) Time-decay (NexCP).} When the drift is gradual and the ratio is
unavailable, geometric weights $w_i=\rho_{\mathrm{decay}}^{\,n-i}$ that
down-weight older calibration points give the non-exchangeable conformal
$p$-value of \citet{barber2023beyond}, whose coverage obeys the one-sided
guarantee $\text{miscoverage}\le\alpha+\sum_i\tilde w_i\,d_{\mathrm{TV},i}$. Both keep the
selection and detection machinery \emph{certified} under quantified drift---the
constructive counterpart of the coverage gap bounded in
Section~\ref{sec:theory}---and we validate them in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Theoretical analysis: when selection starves, and how detection
certifies it}
\label{sec:theory}
We make two things precise: \emph{(i)}~why biasing replay towards
currently-compatible transitions can move the learned fixed point \emph{away}
from the best response, and \emph{(ii)}~in what sense the test martingale of
Section~\ref{sec:detection} \emph{certifies} the selection rule of
Section~\ref{sec:selection}.

\paragraph{Setup.} Fix $\picur$, so the perceived MDP $\mathcal{M}_a(\picur)$ is
stationary (Appendix~\ref{app:br}). Use linear features
$Q_\theta(s,u_a)=\phi(s,u_a)^\top\theta$ with $\Phi$ of full column rank---taken
\emph{tabular or $\mathcal{S}_w$-local} for the cleanest statements below, since a
general $\Phi$ couples in off-support next-state features through the bootstrap. A
buffer measure $\mu$ over $(s,u_a)$ is reweighted by conformal weights
$w_i=g(p_i)\ge0$ (soft $w_i\propto p_i$, or the hard filter
$w_i=\indic{p_i\ge\alpha}$); write the effective $(s,u_a)$-marginal $\dw\propto
w\cdot\mu$, $\Dw=\mathrm{diag}(\dw)$, and the $\Dw$-weighted projection
$\Pi_{\Dw}=\Phi(\Phi^\top\Dw\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top\Dw$. We write the reweighted
operator with the \emph{current} kernel $P^{\picur}$ and reward $\bar r^{\picur}$;
this presupposes the exact-ratio correction (Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation}(iii)).
Under ratio-free scores~\eqref{eq:score-policy} or hard filtering the replayed
$(s',r)$ instead follow $\pipast$, so the effective kernel is the \emph{past}
one---a second bias compounding the support effect below.

\begin{proposition}[Reweighted fixed point]
\label{prop:fixedpoint}
Let $A_w:=\Phi^\top\Dw(I-\gamma P^{\picur})\Phi$ and $b_w=\Phi^\top\Dw\,\bar
r^{\picur}$. If $A_w$ is positive definite, conformal-reweighted semi-gradient TD
converges to the unique fixed point $\theta_w=A_w^{-1}b_w$, solving
$\Phi\theta_w=\Pi_{\Dw}\,\Bop^{\mathrm{BR}}_{\picur}(\Phi\theta_w)$; if $A_w$ is
merely nonsingular the fixed point exists and is unique but the iteration need not
converge to it. A \emph{sufficient} (not necessary~\citep{kolter2011fixed})
condition for $\Pi_{\Dw}\Bop^{\mathrm{BR}}_{\picur}$ to be a $\gamma$-contraction in
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\Dw}$ is that $\dw$ be stationary for $P^{\picur}$; conformal
reweighting deliberately violates it, so contraction---and hence boundedness of the
off-distribution fixed point~\citep{kolter2011fixed,tsitsiklis1997analysis}---is not
guaranteed, and restoring it requires an emphatic
reweighting~\citep{sutton2016emphatic}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Support starvation]
\label{thm:starvation}
Let $\mathcal{S}_w=\supp(\dw)\subseteq\supp(\mu)=\mathcal{S}$, with $\Phi$ tabular
or $\mathcal{S}_w$-local.
\emph{(i)}~$\theta_w$ depends on $\mathcal{M}_a(\picur)$ only through the rows of
$A_w,b_w$ indexed by $\mathcal{S}_w$ (entries off $\mathcal{S}_w$ vanish).
\emph{(ii)}~Hence $\Phi\theta_w$ solves the $\Dw$-weighted projected Bellman
equation supported on $\mathcal{S}_w$---the best response of the sub-MDP retaining
$\mathcal{S}_w$; if an optimal best-response trajectory visits some
$(s,u_a)\notin\mathcal{S}_w$, the greedy policy of $\theta_w$ can be arbitrarily
suboptimal in $\mathcal{M}_a(\picur)$.
\emph{(iii)}~The hard filter $w_i=\indic{p_i\ge\alpha}$ deletes from $\mathcal{S}_w$
every stored $(s,u_a)$ whose $\umai$ is improbable under $\picur$; when the
reward-bearing optimal joint action is currently low-probability (exploration, or
escaping a poor consensus) it is deleted and (ii) applies. Conversely, the exact
score $A=-\log\rho$ with soft weights $w_i\propto\rho_i$ (no truncation) leaves the
$(s,u_a)$-marginal unchanged---$\dw=\mu$, since
$\mathbb{E}_{\pipast}[\rho\mid s,u_a]=1$, so $\mathcal{S}_w=\mathcal{S}$---and
instead replaces the past perceived kernel by the current one inside the Bellman
operator (the importance correction of \citet{foerster2017stabilising}), so
$\theta_w$ targets $\Qbr(\cdot;\picur)$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof: Appendix~\ref{app:starvation}.} The theorem delimits the
\emph{safe regime} of conformal selection---soft, exact-ratio reweighting, which
both preserves support and corrects the kernel, recovering
\citealp{foerster2017stabilising}. Outside it---ratio-free
scores~\eqref{eq:score-policy} or hard $\alpha$-filtering---selection both starves
support \emph{and} leaves the kernel biased towards $\pipast$.

\paragraph{Remark (the Simultaneous-Attack collapse).} In Simultaneous-Attack
(single state; Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) the all-attack joint action is the
only reward-bearing event. Once the iterate drifts towards all-abstain, $\picur$
renders all-attack improbable and the hard filter zeroes its weight, so the rare
exploratory transitions leave $\mathcal{S}_w$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation}(iii)
learning then locks onto the all-abstain consensus---predicting precisely the
regime in which compatibility selection underperforms uniform replay. (With a
single state the trajectory argument of (ii) degenerates; the operative mechanism
is the weight-zeroing of the rewarding arm.)

\begin{lemma}[Detection certifies selection]
\label{lem:certify}
Run the martingale of Section~\ref{sec:detection} on the \emph{same} score $A$ as
the selector, with \emph{fixed} (data-independent) weights $\tilde w_i$---as
required by \citet{barber2023beyond}; the data-dependent rule $w_i\propto p_i$ of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:select} is thus certified only in its frozen-weight form. Model
the windowed $p$-values as i.i.d.\ with density $q$ under drift (uniform under
$H_0$)---an idealisation stronger than the conditional super-uniformity used
elsewhere. Then the beyond-exchangeability coverage gap of the
selector~\citep{barber2023beyond} obeys
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}\ \le\ \sum_i\tilde w_i\,
\dTV\!\big(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{cur}}_A,\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{past}}_A\big)\ \le\
\Big(\textstyle\sum_i\tilde w_i\Big)\sqrt{\tfrac12\,\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})},
\]
and the detector estimates the right-hand side:
$\tfrac1t\log M_t\xrightarrow{\ a.s.\ }\mathbb{E}_q[\log g]
=\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})-\KL(q\,\|\,g)\le\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})$, with equality
for the log-optimal bet $g=q$.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Proof: Appendix~\ref{app:certify}.} A \emph{log-optimal} detector
($g=q$) makes $\tfrac1t\log M_t\to\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})$, an asymptotically
valid \emph{upper} bound on the selector's miscoverage. The deployed power
mixture~\eqref{eq:martingale} is not log-optimal, so its growth rate
\emph{under}-estimates $\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})$ by the regret $\KL(q\,\|\,g)$: it
certifies selection only \emph{qualitatively} (faster growth $\Rightarrow$ larger
drift $\Rightarrow$ wider potential gap). The bound is numerically tight only when
the bet is log-optimal \emph{and} $A=-\log\rho$ (a sufficient statistic of
$\pipast\!\to\!\picur$, so that score drift equals perceived-MDP drift); and the
growth-rate estimate is asymptotic, whereas the alarm~\eqref{eq:ville} is the
anytime-valid object. In this precise sense, \emph{detection certifies selection}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Environments}
\label{sec:envs}
All environments are implemented in JAX, so every method runs inside a single
\texttt{lax.scan} training loop vectorised across seeds. A custom one-step bandit
isolates the mechanism; three cooperative JaxMARL~\cite{rutherford2024jaxmarl}
benchmarks place it in standard settings. The drift-\emph{detection} experiments
use a fixed partner-swap intervention and a calibration set that both assume a
state-independent action set, so they run on the unmasked envs
(Simultaneous-Attack, MPE, Overcooked); SMAX---whose legal actions are
state-dependent---instead exercises the masked-action training target
(Section~\ref{sec:ratio}) and the selection ablation.

\paragraph{Simultaneous-Attack.} A one-step, single-state bandit with $N$ agents
and two actions, \emph{attack} or \emph{abstain}. Attacking together succeeds;
attacking without full participation is ``suicidal''. With $K=\sum_i a_i$
attackers we use the reward
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sim-reward}
r(\mathbf{a})=
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } K=N,\\[2pt]
-\,r_{\mathrm{death}}\,K & \text{if } 0<K<N,\\[2pt]
0 & \text{if } K=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{death}}=\frac{1}{N\,(2^{\,N-1}-1)} .
\end{equation}
This constant (corrected from the earlier $-1/(1-2^{-(N-1)})$, which had the
wrong sign and scale) makes \emph{abstention-vs-coordination} the whole
difficulty: a uniformly random joint policy gets expected return $0$, while the
all-attack optimum gets $1$ (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:reward}). Coordination is
exponentially rare under random play, so the buffer fills with ``failed''
transitions whose value depends entirely on whether teammates would still attack.

\paragraph{MPE \texttt{simple\_spread}.} The standard cooperative-navigation
benchmark~\cite{lowe2017multi} (agents spread to cover landmarks): a multi-state,
partially observable setting in which the perceived dynamics genuinely shift when a
teammate's policy changes---our drift-detection testbed.

\paragraph{SMAX.} The {StarCraft}-style micromanagement
challenge~\cite{samvelyan2019starcraft} reimplemented in JAX, with
\emph{state-dependent action masks}---the regime in which $\rho$ loses support
(Section~\ref{sec:ratio}). It carries the masked-action double-$Q$ target and the
selection ablation.

\paragraph{Overcooked.} A fully-cooperative coordination benchmark in which a
scripted \emph{partner swap} at a known step injects a controlled drift event.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
The backbone is an IQL agent in JAX on top of JaxMARL---a recurrent $Q$-network
with a Flashbax replay buffer, trained with the PureJaxRL
\texttt{scan}+\texttt{vmap} recipe so all seeds run in one compiled call;
$\epsilon$ is annealed from $0.99$ to $0.05$. Replay sampling is a swappable
module---\textsc{uniform}, PER, LaBER, exact-ratio ($A=-\log\rho$), and the
conformal policy/model scores---over a shared loop, so ablations differ only in the
sampler. Conformal scores are calibrated on a fresh on-policy hold-out; the drift
martingale uses the $\varepsilon$-grid mixture at level $\delta=0.05$. We report
\emph{average optimality} (normalised return) for selection and \emph{detection
delay at matched false-alarm} for detection, over $8$ seeds (more on the cheap
bandit), with inter-seed spread shaded.

\subsection{Findings}
\paragraph{Selection by compatibility starves exploration---the negative that
proves the theory.} On Simultaneous-Attack ($N=3$), \textsc{uniform} replay reaches
the optimum on \emph{every} seed at buffer sizes $\{2\mathrm{k},50\mathrm{k},
200\mathrm{k}\}$ (final optimality $1.00$), whereas compatibility selection
\emph{collapses} a fraction of seeds: the ratio-free conformal score falls to
$0.66\pm0.49$ and even the exact ratio $A=-\log\rho$ to $0.57\pm0.51$, both bimodal
($12$ seeds; Figure~\ref{fig:starv}). The conformal machinery is not at fault---the
$p$-values are super-uniform under $H_0$ (Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistic
$\approx0.006$); the cause is precisely Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation}(iii), as
deleting the rare exploratory all-attack transitions pushes the reward-bearing
action out of $\mathcal{S}_w$. We confirm the theorem's \emph{iff} structure
directly ($16$ seeds, support floor $0.3$; Figure~\ref{fig:starv-iff}): selection
recovers the optimum ($1.00$, $0/16$ collapse) \emph{only} when the exact-ratio
score \emph{and} a support floor both hold; dropping either returns to $0.75$
($4/16$)---isolating the theorem's two conditions and ruling out a trivial
``a floor fixes everything'' reading. Across samplers the per-seed correlation
between surviving all-attack mass and final return is $+0.998$. Selection
\emph{by filtering} is thus not the contribution; its certified, support-preserving
forms are (below).

\paragraph{Detection is the robust contribution.} The two-channel controller pairs
a \emph{frozen} calibration channel---whose Ville alarm preserves the anytime
false-alarm guarantee---with an adaptive channel that reads the log-wealth slope
$\mathrm{d}\log M_t/\mathrm{d}t$ ($\approx$ the KL drift rate) to drive the buffer
half-life. The frozen channel is anytime-valid in practice: over $20{,}000$ null
streams the empirical \emph{ever}-cross rate is $0.040$ at $\delta=0.1$ and $0.019$
at $\delta=0.05$, at or below the Ville bound~\eqref{eq:ville}. On MPE
\texttt{simple\_spread}, after a controlled partner-policy swap, the conformal
martingale detects $8/8$ seeds at delay $21$--$29$ (mean $25.5$) at \emph{zero}
false alarms, using a \emph{single threshold transferred} from the
Simultaneous-Attack false-alarm lab. The TD-error heuristic
AES~\cite{wang2026tracking}, held to that \emph{same} transferable threshold, fires
in only $4/8$ seeds; with a task-local reference it fires in $0/8$, and only under
full per-environment recalibration does it reach $6/8$---then slowly (median delay
$86$) and still missing two seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:det-mpe}). Conformal detection
thus pairs an anytime guarantee with a threshold that \emph{transfers}, the
asymmetry Lemma~\ref{lem:certify} anticipates. The Overcooked partner-swap
corroborates this ($7/8$, median delay ${\approx}120$) at a smaller training budget
(Figure~\ref{fig:det-oc}).

\paragraph{Certified replay recovers what filtering loses.} Where $\rho$ is
available, $\rho$-weighted conformal $p$-values~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal} give
the exact-ratio score its first finite-sample validity under the known co-action
shift---the ``missing middle'' between the ratio-free heuristic and raw importance
sampling---and time-decay NexCP~\cite{barber2023beyond} turns Foerster's heuristic
discount of stale data into one whose one-sided coverage gap vanishes with the
drift. On controlled score streams with a \emph{known} shift the gains are exact:
under a covariate shift the ordinary inductive $p$-value is badly miscalibrated
($\mathbb{P}(p\le0.05)=0.26$, a $5.2\times$ inflation), whereas the
$\rho$-weighted $p$-value~\eqref{eq:weighted-p} restores it to $0.04$ (mean $p$:
$0.24\!\to\!0.50$); for a gradually drifting stream, plain exchangeable conformal
under-covers at $30.5\%$ against the $80\%$ nominal while time-decay NexCP
weighting restores $85.8\%$, the one-sided bound holding throughout. Both are
super-uniform with coverage tending to the nominal level as drift$\,\to0$, matching
Lemma~\ref{lem:certify}.

\section{Related Work}
The non-stationarity of the perceived environment is a long-standing theme in
cooperative MARL~\cite{matignon2012independent}. \citet{foerster2017stabilising}
stabilise experience replay by conditioning on a low-dimensional fingerprint of
training time and by importance-weighting the loss; we revisit their ratio,
correct its formulation, and remove its dependence on queryable discrete
policies. Value-decomposition methods such as
VDN~\cite{sunehag2018value} and QMIX~\cite{rashid2018qmix}, and the actor-critic
MADDPG~\cite{lowe2017multi}, address credit assignment but still inherit
non-stationary individual learners; PPO-based on-policy
methods~\cite{yu2022surprising} sidestep replay at a sample-efficiency cost.
On the replay side, prioritised experience
replay~\cite{schaul2016prioritized} and LaBER~\cite{lahire2022large} reshape the
sampling distribution by TD error or gradient norm; we borrow their
large-batch machinery but prioritise by \emph{policy conformity} rather than by
error magnitude. Our detection component builds on conformal
prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle} and on conformal
test martingales for change
detection~\cite{vovk2003testing,volkhonskiy2017inductive}, whose anytime validity
follows from Ville's inequality~\cite{ville1939etude,ramdas2023gametheoretic}.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We recast the reuse of past experience in off-policy MARL as a question of
\emph{conformity to the present} and answered it with conformal prediction. A
single non-conformity score, calibrated into distribution-free $p$-values,
\emph{detects} drift of the perceived environment through a conformal test
martingale---with an anytime false-alarm guarantee and a threshold that transfers
across environments---and \emph{certifies} the replayed batch under a quantified
shift via weighted and time-decayed $p$-values. The same score can also
\emph{select} compatible transitions, but here our message is deliberately
cautionary: we prove, and confirm empirically, that outside the exact-ratio
regime selection starves the support it needs and underperforms uniform replay.
Detection and certification are thus the robust contributions; selection is safe
only in the narrow regime where it coincides with the (often unavailable)
importance ratio.

\balance

Several directions remain. The immediate next step is full-scale convergence on
the masked / partially observable testbeds---SMAX and Overcooked---where the
importance ratio loses support and our stored-state GRU detector already runs;
these runs are GPU-bound and in progress. The state-visitation mismatch is
untouched and could be addressed with state-conditional (Mondrian) calibration or
corrections on $d(s)$; validity in the dependent RL stream deserves a dedicated
analysis via e-processes~\cite{ramdas2023gametheoretic}; and value-decomposition
backbones such as VDN and QMIX inherit the same per-agent score unchanged.

\section{Convergence of $Q$-learning, corrected}
\label{app:contraction}
The optimal Bellman operator \eqref{eq:bellman} maps an \emph{arbitrary} $Q$ to a
new function (it uses $Q$ on the right-hand side, not $Q^*$). It is a
$\gamma$-contraction in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$:
\begin{align*}
\big|(\Bop^*Q_1)(s,a)-(\Bop^*Q_2)(s,a)\big|
&=\gamma\Big|\mathbb{E}_{s'}\big[\textstyle\max_{a'}Q_1(s',a')-\max_{a'}Q_2(s',a')\big]\Big|\\
&\le\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{s'}\big[\textstyle\max_{a'}|Q_1(s',a')-Q_2(s',a')|\big]\\
&\le\gamma\,\lVert Q_1-Q_2\rVert_\infty,
\end{align*}
using $|\max_x f(x)-\max_x g(x)|\le\max_x|f(x)-g(x)|$. Hence
$\lVert\Bop^*Q_1-\Bop^*Q_2\rVert_\infty\le\gamma\lVert Q_1-Q_2\rVert_\infty$ with
$\gamma<1$; the factor $\gamma$ is what makes it a contraction (a
\emph{non-expansion} would not suffice for Banach's theorem). The fixed point is
$Q^*$.

\section{The perceived best-response operator}
\label{app:br}
For fixed $\pi_{-a}$, the operator $\Bop^{\mathrm{BR}}_{\pi_{-a}}$ of
\eqref{eq:br} is a $\gamma$-contraction: the others' actions are averaged out
into the perceived kernel $P^{\pi_{-a}}$ and reward $\bar r^{\pi_{-a}}$, reducing
\eqref{eq:br} to an ordinary optimal Bellman operator on $\mathcal{M}_a(\pi_{-a})$,
to which Appendix~\ref{app:contraction} applies verbatim. Its fixed point
$\Qbr(\cdot;\pi_{-a})$ is the best response of $a$ to $\pi_{-a}$. The MARL Bellman
optimality equation
\[
\Qbr(s,u_a;\picur)=
\mathbb{E}_{\uma\sim\picur}\Big[r_a(s,\mathbf{u})+\gamma\sum_{s'}\mathcal{T}(s'\mid s,\mathbf{u})\max_{u_a'}\Qbr(s',u_a';\picur)\Big]
\]
must be read as a best-response value \emph{conditional on the current others'
policies}, under the quasi-stationary approximation that they are held fixed
during the update---not as a joint-optimal or equilibrium value.

\section{Simultaneous-Attack reward, corrected}
\label{app:reward}
Let each agent attack independently with probability $\tfrac12$ (uniform random
policy), so $K=\sum_i a_i\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,\tfrac12)$. With the reward
\eqref{eq:sim-reward},
\[
\mathbb{E}_{U}[r]=\underbrace{1\cdot\mathbb{P}(K=N)}_{=\,2^{-N}}
-\,r_{\mathrm{death}}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}k\,\mathbb{P}(K=k).
\]
Since $\sum_{k=0}^{N}k\,\mathbb{P}(K=k)=\mathbb{E}[K]=N/2$ and the $k=N$ term is
$N\cdot2^{-N}$,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}k\,\mathbb{P}(K=k)=\frac N2-\frac{N}{2^{N}}
=\frac N2\Big(1-2^{-(N-1)}\Big)=\frac{N\,(2^{N-1}-1)}{2^{N}} .
\]
Therefore $\mathbb{E}_U[r]=2^{-N}-r_{\mathrm{death}}\cdot N(2^{N-1}-1)\,2^{-N}$,
which vanishes exactly for
\[
r_{\mathrm{death}}=\frac{1}{N\,(2^{N-1}-1)}>0,
\]
giving a genuine \emph{penalty} ($-r_{\mathrm{death}}K<0$) for partial attacks,
$\mathbb{E}_U[r]=0$ for random play, and $r=1$ for the all-attack optimum. (For
$N=2$: $r_{\mathrm{death}}=\tfrac12$, and
$\mathbb{E}_U[r]=\tfrac14-\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=0$.)

\section{Conformal martingale validity}
\label{app:martingale}
Let $\mathcal{F}_t=\sigma(p_1,\dots,p_t)$. If, under $H_0$, each $p_t$ is
conditionally super-uniform, $\mathbb{P}(p_t\le u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\le u$ for
all $u\in[0,1]$, then for $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$,
\[
\mathbb{E}\big[\varepsilon p_t^{\varepsilon-1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]
\le\int_0^1\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon-1}\,\mathrm{d}u=1,
\]
so $M_t^{\varepsilon}=\prod_{j\le t}\varepsilon p_j^{\varepsilon-1}$ is a
non-negative supermartingale with $M_0=1$ (a martingale when the $p_t$ are exactly
uniform). Mixing over $\varepsilon$ preserves this. Ville's
inequality~\cite{ville1939etude} for non-negative supermartingales gives
$\mathbb{P}_{H_0}(\sup_{t}M_t\ge1/\delta)\le\mathbb{E}[M_0]\,\delta=\delta$,
which is \eqref{eq:ville} and justifies the anytime threshold $M_t\ge1/\delta$.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:starvation} (support starvation)}
\label{app:starvation}
Take $\Phi$ tabular or $\mathcal{S}_w$-local, so $\Pi_{\Dw}$ does not couple in
features of states outside $\mathcal{S}_w$; for a general $\Phi$ the bootstrap term
$\gamma\Phi^\top\Dw P^{\picur}\Phi$ carries next-state features $\phi(l)$ with
$l\notin\mathcal{S}_w$, and the clean ``sub-MDP'' reading of (ii) holds only when
$\mathcal{S}_w$ is closed under $P^{\picur}$.
\emph{(i)} Both $A_w=\Phi^\top\Dw(I-\gamma P^{\picur})\Phi$ and
$b_w=\Phi^\top\Dw\,\bar r^{\picur}$ carry $\Dw=\mathrm{diag}(\dw)$ as a left
factor; any $(s,u_a)$ with $\dw(s,u_a)=0$ contributes a zero row to $\Dw$ and so
does not enter $A_w$ or $b_w$. Hence $\theta_w$ is determined by
$\{P^{\picur},\bar r^{\picur}\}$ only through the rows indexed by $\mathcal{S}_w$.
\emph{(ii)} On $\mathcal{S}_w$ the normal equation $A_w\theta_w=b_w$ is the
$\Dw$-weighted projected Bellman equation of the sub-MDP that retains only
state-actions in $\mathcal{S}_w$, whose solution is that sub-MDP's best-response
value. Two perceived MDPs that agree on $\mathcal{S}_w$ but differ on
$\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}_w$ yield identical $A_w,b_w$, hence identical
$\theta_w$, while their true $\Qbr$ differ; choosing the off-support values so that
an omitted action is optimal makes $\argmax_{u_a}\phi(s,u_a)^\top\theta_w$
suboptimal, with a gap as large as the reward range permits.
\emph{(iii)} For the hard filter $\dw(s,u_a)\propto\mu(s,u_a)\,\indic{p\ge\alpha}$,
and $p$ is small exactly when $\umai$ is improbable under $\picur$ (small
$\picur(\umai\mid s)$, large score $A$); such pairs receive $\dw=0$ and leave
$\mathcal{S}_w$. For the converse, $\rho$ is a function of $\uma$ (not of
$(s,u_a)$) with $\mathbb{E}_{\pipast}[\rho\mid s,u_a]=1$, so $w_i\propto\rho_i$
leaves the $(s,u_a)$-marginal unchanged ($\dw=\mu$, $\mathcal{S}_w=\mathcal{S}$)
while, within each $(s,u_a)$, re-weighting the stored $(\uma,s',r)$ from $\pipast$
to $\picur$---replacing the past perceived kernel by the current one, on which the
ordinary contraction of Appendix~\ref{app:br} applies and $\theta_w$ targets
$\Qbr(\cdot;\picur)$.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:certify} (detection certifies selection)}
\label{app:certify}
\citet{barber2023beyond}, extending weighted conformal
prediction~\citep{tibshirani2019conformal}, bound the coverage gap with
\emph{fixed} weights at the \emph{residual} (score) level:
$\text{coverage}\ge1-\alpha-\sum_i\tilde w_i\,\dTV\!\big(R(Z),R(Z^{(i)})\big)$,
where $R$ is the score map and $Z^{(i)}$ swaps calibration point $i$ with the test
point. With $R=A$ and the windowed i.i.d.\ model each swap perturbs a single
coordinate, so $\dTV(R(Z),R(Z^{(i)}))=\dTV(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{cur}}_A,
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{past}}_A)$ and the bound collapses to the Lemma's first
inequality. Let $F_{\mathrm{past}}$ be the (continuous, tie-free) past score CDF,
so $p=1-F_{\mathrm{past}}(A)$ is a monotone, invertible probability integral
transform: under $H_0$, $p\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$; under drift its law has density
$q$. As $\KL$ and total variation are invariant under invertible maps,
$\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})=\KL(\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{cur}}_A\,\|\,\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{past}}_A)$
and likewise for $\dTV$; Pinsker's inequality $\dTV\le\sqrt{\tfrac12\KL}$ then gives
the second inequality. For the growth rate,
$\tfrac1t\log M_t=\tfrac1t\sum_{j\le t}\log g(p_j)\to\mathbb{E}_q[\log g(p)]$ a.s.\
(SLLN), and
$\mathbb{E}_q[\log g]=\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})-\KL(q\,\|\,g)\le\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})$,
with equality iff $g=q$. Hence only the log-optimal bet makes
$\tfrac1t\log M_t\to\KL(q\,\|\,\mathrm{Unif})$ and turns the plug-in into an
(asymptotic) \emph{upper} bound on $\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}$; the power
mixture~\eqref{eq:martingale}, equal to $q$ only when $q$ lies in the family
$\{\varepsilon p^{\varepsilon-1}\}$, under-estimates it by the regret
$\KL(q\,\|\,g)$ and certifies only qualitatively. The bound also requires fixed
weights (the data-dependent selector $w_i\propto p_i$ falls outside the
hypothesis), and equality of score drift with perceived-MDP drift requires $A$ to
be a sufficient statistic of $\pipast\!\to\!\picur$, i.e.\ $A=-\log\rho$. The
estimate is asymptotic; the alarm~\eqref{eq:ville} remains the anytime-valid
object.
